The break-even map

Every free tier in the stack, placed at the number of active parents where it stops being free. Log scale, because the answers are four orders of magnitude apart.

WhatsApp is free, and that is the positioning working

Service messages sent inside WhatsApp's 24-hour customer-service window cost nothing. This product is purely responsive — the parent asks, we answer, and even the post-answer prompt “क्या आपने बच्चे को समझा दिया?” lands inside the same window. So the per-message fee in the cost formula is zero by design, not by luck: the decision to be a coach who responds rather than a service that broadcasts is also the decision that deletes a whole line from the cost model.

Corrected

The Devanagari tax is real, and smaller than I first claimed

Token counts were first inferred three ways that disagreed by 1.6× — the rate limiter's messages, a local tokenizer, and implied consumption from exhausting the daily budget. The local tokenizer was wrong: it over-counted prompt tokens by 1.8×, because the model actually in use tokenises Devanagari considerably better than the one I had loaded.

Capturing the provider's own usage accounting — free, and something the client should have recorded from the start — settled it at 2,213 tokens per answer. The real Hindi penalty is 1.75 characters per token against ~4 for English, about 2.3×, not the 3.7× first reported. A Hindi product does pay a structural token premium; it is just not as steep as the wrong measurement suggested.

What this model cannot tell you

  • Bhashini's commercial rates are not published. Free for non-commercial use, and ₹250/month for 50,000 TTS characters a day on the published individual plan — about 143 answers at our 348 characters. Past that, "contact Bhashini". This is the largest unknown, and note the shape of the risk: at district scale the voice layer could plausibly exceed the LLM layer, and it is the one line that cannot be modelled from public information.
  • Speech duration is assumed, not measured. 20 seconds per voice note comes from the spec. There is no voice loop built yet, so there is nothing to measure.
  • The model in use has no published price. Qwen3-32B ($0.29/$0.59 per million) stands in as the nearest listed equivalent. A smaller model is likely cheaper, so these figures probably overstate cost.
  • Evaluation competes with the product for the same budget. A 30-question conformance run costs ~66,000 tokens — a third of the free daily allowance — so the spec's "re-run on every change" cadence is not affordable as written. The retrieval, gate, audit and cost evaluations need no model at all, and carry that cadence instead.
